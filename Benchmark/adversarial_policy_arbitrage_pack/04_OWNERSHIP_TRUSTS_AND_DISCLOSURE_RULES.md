# Norland Ownership, Trusts, and Beneficial Disclosure Rules 2032

**Instrument:** OTBDR-2032  
**Effective date:** 1 January 2032  
**Registry:** Norland Beneficial Ownership Office

> Fictional rules designed to test indirect ownership, nominee arrangements, trusts, control, disclosure, and anti-circumvention reasoning.

## 1. Beneficial owner

A beneficial owner is a natural person who directly or indirectly:

- owns 25% or more of voting or economic rights;
- can appoint or remove a majority of the governing body;
- exercises control through contract, financing, family arrangement, veto, or coordinated action; or
- is entitled to 25% or more of distributions from a trust or similar arrangement.

Where no person meets a threshold, the senior managing official is reported, without implying economic ownership.

## 2. Indirect ownership

Percentage ownership is multiplied through each ownership chain and added across chains. Rights held by a nominee for another person are treated as held by the principal. Options exercisable within 12 months are treated as exercised if their principal purpose is to affect disclosure or eligibility thresholds.

## 3. Family attribution

Spousal ownership is not automatically aggregated for registry percentage calculations if each spouse acquired the interest with independent funds, exercises independent voting, and has no agreement to coordinate. However, coordinated action, shared financing, reciprocal options, or a common economic arrangement may establish joint control.

Minor-child holdings are attributed to the parent or guardian who funded or controls them.

## 4. Trusts

The reportable persons of a trust include the settlor, trustees, any protector with removal or veto powers, beneficiaries entitled to 25% or more, and any person exercising ultimate effective control.

A discretionary beneficiary with no fixed entitlement is reportable only if the beneficiary has received at least 25% of trust distributions during the preceding three years or exercises control. A letter of wishes is considered evidence but is not conclusive.

## 5. Temporary transfers

A transfer is ignored for disclosure and eligibility calculations if there is a binding or informal understanding that the interest will be returned, the transferor retains economic exposure, or the transferee is protected against loss. A genuine outright transfer is respected even if motivated partly by eligibility, provided the transferor retains no control, benefit, or reversal right.

## 6. Debt and control

An ordinary lender is not an owner merely because of financial covenants. A lender is treated as exercising control where it can direct ordinary business decisions, appoint a majority of directors outside default, or receive substantially all residual profits.

Rights exercisable only after genuine default are disregarded until default, unless default was engineered to activate them.

## 7. Disclosure timing

A reporting entity must update the registry within 30 calendar days after becoming aware, or when it reasonably should have become aware, of a reportable change.

An incomplete filing made on time does not stop the deadline unless it identifies the missing information, explains reasonable steps taken, and supplies the information within 15 additional days.

## 8. Programme look-through

Where another Norland law uses ownership, common control, related-person, or group thresholds, this instrument supplies the ownership calculation unless that law provides a more specific rule. The substantive program may impose broader aggregation based on practical control.

## 9. Anti-circumvention

The Registry and a program administrator may disregard arrangements that have no substantial purpose other than avoiding disclosure or an ownership threshold. Relevant facts include side letters, synchronized voting, put-and-call pairs, matched financing, indemnities, temporary transfers, and continued enjoyment of distributions.

Tax or program eligibility motivation does not alone invalidate a genuine transfer. The decisive question is whether legal rights, economic exposure, control, and practical conduct changed consistently with the asserted transfer.

## 10. Confidential nominees and advisers

Lawyers, accountants, formation agents, and professional nominees must identify the person for whom they act. Professional status does not shield beneficial ownership. An adviser who merely gives independent advice is not an owner or controller.

## 11. Penalties and correction

A person who discovers an innocent error must correct it within 20 days. Timely self-correction generally prevents a penalty. Deliberate concealment, fabricated independence, or false nominee declarations may trigger civil penalties and referral for prosecution under fictional Norland law.
