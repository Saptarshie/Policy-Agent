# Republic of Norland Consolidated Revenue Code 2032

**Instrument:** Revenue Code RC-2032  
**Jurisdiction:** Republic of Norland, a fictional jurisdiction  
**Effective date:** 1 January 2032  
**Purpose:** Synthetic adversarial reasoning benchmark

> This is fictional law for model evaluation. It must not be treated as tax, legal, investment, or accounting advice. The Code deliberately combines safe harbors, anti-avoidance rules, attribution rules, timing provisions, and substance tests.

## 1. Core concepts

### 1.1 Tax year

The ordinary tax year is the calendar year. A registered business may elect a 31 March year-end before its first return. An election cannot be changed for five years without Revenue Authority approval.

### 1.2 Residence

An individual is resident if present in Norland for at least 183 days in the tax year, or if the individual's permanent home and center of vital interests are in Norland. A company is resident if incorporated in Norland or centrally managed and controlled there.

### 1.3 Related persons

Related persons include spouses, domestic partners, ancestors, descendants, siblings, entities under common control, and any person acting according to another's directions. Ownership held by a spouse or minor child is attributed to the individual unless the spouse proves independent funding, control, and economic benefit.

## 2. Progressive individual tax

| Taxable income band | Rate |
|---|---:|
| First N$500,000 | 10% |
| N$500,001–N$1,500,000 | 20% |
| Above N$1,500,000 | 35% |

Taxable income includes salary, business income, rent, interest, disguised remuneration, and gains recharacterized under this Code.

## 3. Employment and contractor classification

### 3.1 Employee remuneration

Salary, bonuses, allowances, benefits provided because of employment, and amounts redirected at an employee's request are taxable to the employee when paid or made available.

### 3.2 Personal service entities

Income earned by a company or partnership is attributed to the individual performing the services when all of the following apply:

1. More than 70% of the entity's service revenue for the year comes from one client and its related persons.
2. The individual's skill or labor is the principal source of that revenue.
3. The entity has fewer than three unrelated full-time employees who perform substantive revenue-producing work.

Attribution does not apply where the entity bears meaningful commercial risk, owns substantial non-personal assets used in delivery, and can substitute qualified personnel without client approval. Labels in invoices or contracts do not control.

### 3.3 Deferred compensation

A right to remuneration is taxed when it becomes unconditionally available, even if the individual asks the payer to defer transfer. A genuine forfeitable award subject to substantial future service conditions is taxed when the condition lapses.

## 4. Business deductions

### 4.1 General rule

A business may deduct an expense only to the extent incurred wholly and exclusively to produce taxable business income. Mixed expenses must be reasonably apportioned.

### 4.2 Capital items

Land, buildings, vehicles, computers, and equipment expected to provide benefit for more than one year are capital items. They are not immediately deductible unless a specific allowance applies.

### 4.3 Small-asset allowance

A business may immediately deduct qualifying equipment costing no more than N$100,000 per item. Functionally integrated components acquired under a single plan are treated as one item. Splitting an invoice, purchase order, or ownership title does not create separate items.

### 4.4 Home workspace

A proportion of rent and utilities is deductible where a distinct area is used regularly and exclusively for business. A room used personally for more than 20 days during the year is not exclusive. Temporary personal use caused by a declared emergency does not break exclusivity.

### 4.5 Family payments

Compensation paid to a related person is deductible only to the extent it is commercially reasonable for documented services actually performed. Excess amounts are treated as nondeductible distributions to the owner. The recipient is taxed on the amount actually received unless another provision applies.

## 5. Investment entities

### 5.1 Family Investment Company

A resident Family Investment Company (FIC) pays 18% tax on passive investment income. Dividends paid to resident individual shareholders carry a credit for FIC tax already paid.

### 5.2 Retained earnings surcharge

An FIC owes a 12% surcharge on passive income retained more than 24 months after the year in which it arose, unless the retention supports a documented investment commitment entered before the end of that 24-month period.

A commitment to a related entity counts only if independently priced, commercially enforceable, and substantially performed within 12 months. Circular transfers, revocable commitments, and arrangements whose principal effect is delay do not qualify.

### 5.3 Shareholder loans

A loan by an FIC to a shareholder or related person is treated as a dividend at the end of the tax year unless, before payment, it is governed by a written agreement with a market interest rate, fixed repayment schedule not exceeding three years, adequate security for loans above N$500,000, and at least one scheduled principal payment made by year-end.

Repayment funded directly or indirectly by a substantially identical new loan from the FIC within 90 days is ignored.

## 6. Capital gains and loss rules

### 6.1 Rate

Net capital gains are taxed at 15% unless recharacterized as ordinary income.

### 6.2 Short holding and business activity

Gain from property held fewer than 180 days is ordinary income if the taxpayer acquired it with a dominant resale purpose. Repeated similar transactions may establish a trading business regardless of holding period.

### 6.3 Loss harvesting

A realized capital loss is denied if the taxpayer or a related person acquires the same or substantially identical property within 30 days before or after the disposal. The denied loss is added to the replacement property's basis.

Property is substantially identical where economic exposure, issuer risk, maturity, and payoff are materially the same. Broad-market funds tracking different independently maintained indices are not substantially identical merely because they are correlated.

### 6.4 Bed-and-spouse rule

A sale by one spouse followed by purchase by the other spouse is subject to Section 6.3 through related-person attribution. Independent bank accounts do not by themselves prevent attribution.

## 7. Donations and public-benefit credits

A taxpayer receives a 25% credit for cash donated to a registered Public Benefit Organization (PBO), limited to 20% of pre-credit tax liability. No credit arises to the extent the taxpayer or a related person receives a material benefit, reimbursement, side payment, preferential contract, or asset transfer connected with the donation.

Naming recognition and benefits worth no more than 2% of the donation are disregarded. Benefits above 2% reduce the qualifying donation by their fair value; deliberate concealment makes the entire connected donation ineligible.

## 8. Startup investment relief

An individual subscribing new cash for ordinary shares in a certified startup receives a credit equal to 30% of the subscription, capped at N$300,000 per year. The shares must be held for three years.

Relief is recaptured if, during the three-year period, the investor or a related person receives capital repayment, a guaranteed exit, downside protection covering more than 20% of invested capital, or a loan funded by the startup or its promoter.

Ordinary commercial dividends do not trigger recapture. A genuine bank loan on market terms, not guaranteed or funded by the startup or promoter, does not disqualify the investment.

## 9. General Anti-Avoidance Rule

### 9.1 Abusive arrangement

The Revenue Authority may disregard, combine, or recharacterize steps where:

1. A tax advantage is a principal purpose of the arrangement;
2. The arrangement defeats the object or purpose of a provision; and
3. The arrangement lacks commercial substance or uses steps that would not reasonably be taken apart from the tax advantage.

### 9.2 Indicators

Indicators include circular cash flows, offsetting obligations, pre-arranged reversals, accommodating parties, inconsistent legal and economic ownership, artificial fragmentation, and a return materially insulated from commercial risk.

### 9.3 Safe tax planning

Choosing between alternatives expressly contemplated by the Code is not abusive merely because tax influenced the choice. A transaction with genuine commercial substance is not protected if artificial steps were inserted solely to obtain an unintended advantage; only the artificial steps may be disregarded where separable.

### 9.4 Burden and disclosure

The Authority must identify the tax advantage and statutory purpose allegedly defeated. A taxpayer relying on a safe harbor must establish its factual conditions. Full disclosure reduces penalties but does not validate an abusive arrangement.

## 10. Timing, notices, and corrections

Returns are due 90 days after year-end. A taxpayer may amend within two years. The Authority may assess within four years, extended to six years for substantial omission and without limit for fraud. A disclosed position supported by a reasonable interpretation carries no negligence penalty even if rejected.
