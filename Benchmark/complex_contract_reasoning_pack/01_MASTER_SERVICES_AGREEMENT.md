# Alderstone Analytics and BlueHarbor Retail Master Services Agreement

**Agreement ID:** MSA-AB-2026  
**Effective Date:** 15 January 2026  
**Parties:** Alderstone Analytics Private Limited ("Provider") and BlueHarbor Retail Limited ("Customer")  
**Status:** Executed fictional agreement for model evaluation

> This is a synthetic legal document created solely for testing retrieval, multi-document reasoning, clause reconciliation, and contractual analysis. It is not legal advice.

## 1. Agreement Structure and Priority

### 1.1 Contract Documents

The agreement consists of this Master Services Agreement (MSA), each executed Order Form, the Service Level Schedule (SLS), the Data Processing Addendum (DPA), and any signed Change Order.

### 1.2 Order of Precedence

If provisions conflict, the following order controls, from highest to lowest priority:

1. A signed Change Order, but only for the subjects it expressly identifies as amended.
2. The DPA, but only for privacy, security, personal-data processing, audit, breach, and cross-border transfer matters.
3. The applicable Order Form.
4. The SLS.
5. This MSA.

A document does not override another merely because it is signed later. It must either rank higher under this section or expressly identify the provision being amended.

### 1.3 No Implied Amendment

Purchase orders, emails, tickets, meeting notes, product documentation, and portal entries are administrative only and cannot amend the agreement. Course of dealing and failure to enforce a provision do not waive it.

## 2. Services and Customer Dependencies

### 2.1 Services

Provider will supply the hosted analytics platform, implementation, support, and other services identified in each Order Form. Provider may improve the service provided that it does not materially reduce the contracted core functionality during an active subscription term.

### 2.2 Customer Cooperation

Customer must provide timely access to personnel, systems, data, credentials, testing environments, approvals, and accurate requirements. Dates affected by a Customer-Caused Delay move day-for-day, plus a reasonable remobilization period not exceeding five business days.

### 2.3 Customer-Caused Delay

A delay is Customer-Caused only if Provider: (a) identifies the missing dependency in writing, (b) states the reasonably expected impact, and (c) gives Customer at least three business days to cure, unless the dependency was due immediately under an agreed project plan. Provider may not classify a delay as Customer-Caused after the affected milestone has already been missed.

### 2.4 Subcontractors

Provider may use subcontractors but remains responsible for their performance. Subprocessors handling personal data are governed by the DPA. Customer approval is not required for ordinary subcontractors unless an Order Form expressly states otherwise.

## 3. Fees, Invoicing, and Disputed Amounts

### 3.1 Fees

Customer will pay the fees in each Order Form. Except for usage fees, taxes, approved pass-through expenses, and fees changed through a valid adjustment clause, fees are fixed during the Initial Term.

### 3.2 Invoices and Payment

Provider invoices subscription fees annually in advance and usage fees monthly in arrears. Undisputed amounts are due within 30 calendar days after receipt of a valid invoice.

### 3.3 Good-Faith Disputes

Customer may withhold only the disputed portion if it gives detailed written notice within 15 calendar days after invoice receipt. The parties must meet within ten business days. Customer must pay all undisputed portions on time. No late fee or suspension applies to a properly disputed amount while the dispute process is followed.

### 3.4 Late Payment and Suspension

Undisputed late amounts accrue interest at 1% per month or the maximum lawful rate, whichever is lower. Provider may suspend affected services only after giving ten business days' written notice and an additional five-business-day cure period. Provider may not suspend for disputed amounts or where its invoice lacks the information required by the applicable Order Form.

### 3.5 Setoff

Neither party may set off amounts except for: (a) finally adjudicated amounts, (b) amounts admitted in writing, or (c) service credits expressly permitted to be applied against an invoice under the SLS.

## 4. Term and Renewal

### 4.1 MSA Term

The MSA starts on the Effective Date and continues while any Order Form remains active.

### 4.2 Order Terms

Each Order Form states its Initial Term. Unless an Order Form says otherwise, it automatically renews for successive 12-month periods unless either party gives at least 60 calendar days' written notice before the current term ends.

### 4.3 Renewal Pricing

At renewal, Provider may increase recurring subscription fees by the lesser of: (a) 5%, or (b) the year-over-year percentage increase in the India Consumer Price Index, All Groups, published for the most recent available month before notice. Provider must give at least 75 days' notice. If notice is late, the increase takes effect at the following renewal, not the imminent renewal.

## 5. Warranties

### 5.1 Performance Warranty

Provider warrants that the service will materially conform to the documentation and that professional services will be performed with reasonable skill and care. Customer's exclusive remedy is correction or re-performance; if Provider cannot cure a material breach within 30 days after valid notice, Customer may terminate the affected service and receive a pro-rata refund of prepaid unused fees.

### 5.2 Malware

Provider will use commercially reasonable measures designed to prevent malicious code introduced by Provider. This warranty does not apply to code introduced through Customer systems, Customer instructions, or third-party integrations not supplied by Provider.

### 5.3 Disclaimer

Except as expressly stated, the services are provided without implied warranties to the maximum extent permitted by law. Provider does not warrant that every analysis or prediction will be error-free or suitable for decisions requiring human review.

## 6. Confidentiality

### 6.1 Confidential Information

Confidential Information includes nonpublic commercial, technical, security, financial, product, customer, and personal information disclosed in any form and information that should reasonably be understood as confidential.

### 6.2 Exclusions

Information is not confidential if the recipient proves it: (a) was lawfully known without duty, (b) becomes public without breach, (c) is independently developed without use of the information, or (d) is lawfully received from a third party without restriction.

### 6.3 Required Disclosure

A recipient compelled by law to disclose Confidential Information must, where legally permitted, give prompt notice and reasonable assistance. It may disclose only the legally required portion.

### 6.4 Duration

Confidentiality obligations last five years after disclosure, except obligations for trade secrets and personal data continue as long as the information remains protected by applicable law or the DPA.

## 7. Intellectual Property

### 7.1 Background Materials

Each party retains its pre-existing technology, tools, templates, methods, data, know-how, and improvements not uniquely created for the other party.

### 7.2 Customer Data

Customer owns Customer Data. Customer grants Provider a limited license to process Customer Data only to provide, secure, support, and improve the services as allowed by the DPA.

### 7.3 Deliverables

Unless an Order Form expressly identifies a deliverable as "Customer-Owned Custom Work," Provider owns all deliverables, configurations, connectors, scripts, models, and documentation developed under the agreement. Customer receives a non-exclusive, worldwide license to use such deliverables during the applicable subscription term for its internal business operations.

### 7.4 Customer-Owned Custom Work

For an item expressly labeled Customer-Owned Custom Work, ownership transfers after full payment. Provider retains ownership of embedded Background Materials and grants Customer a perpetual license to those embedded materials only as necessary to use the custom work.

### 7.5 Feedback and Aggregated Data

Provider may use feedback without restriction. Provider may create and use aggregated or de-identified data only if it cannot reasonably identify Customer, an individual, or Customer's clients, and only subject to the DPA.

## 8. Indemnities

### 8.1 Provider IP Indemnity

Provider will defend Customer against third-party claims that the unmodified paid service infringes a patent, copyright, or trademark, and will pay finally awarded damages or approved settlements. Provider has no obligation for claims arising from Customer Data, unauthorized modification, combination with items not supplied by Provider where the claim would not otherwise arise, or continued use after Provider offers a non-infringing replacement.

### 8.2 Customer Indemnity

Customer will defend Provider against third-party claims arising from Customer Data, Customer's unlawful use, or Customer instructions that violate law or third-party rights.

### 8.3 Process

The indemnified party must provide prompt notice, reasonable cooperation, and sole control of defense to the indemnifying party. A delayed notice reduces obligations only to the extent of actual material prejudice. No settlement may admit fault, impose nonmonetary obligations, or restrict business operations of the indemnified party without written consent.

## 9. Limitation of Liability

### 9.1 General Cap

Except for Excluded Claims, each party's aggregate liability arising from an Order Form is capped at fees paid or payable under that Order Form during the 12 months immediately preceding the event first giving rise to the claim.

### 9.2 Enhanced Cap

Liability for breach of confidentiality, data-protection obligations, or indemnity obligations is capped at two times the General Cap.

### 9.3 Excluded Claims

The caps do not apply to fraud, willful misconduct, death or personal injury caused by negligence where liability cannot lawfully be limited, Customer's payment obligations for services properly provided, or infringement caused by a party's unauthorized use of the other party's intellectual property.

### 9.4 Consequential Loss

Neither party is liable for indirect, incidental, special, punitive, or consequential damages, or lost profits, revenues, goodwill, or anticipated savings. This exclusion does not prevent recovery of: (a) amounts payable to a third party under an indemnified claim, (b) reasonable breach notification and identity-protection costs under the DPA, or (c) service credits.

### 9.5 Multiple Claims

Related claims arising from the same or substantially related facts are treated as one claim arising on the date of the earliest event.

## 10. Termination

### 10.1 Material Breach

Either party may terminate an affected Order Form for material breach not cured within 30 days after detailed written notice. Nonpayment of an undisputed amount has a ten-business-day cure period after the notice required by Section 3.4.

### 10.2 Insolvency

Either party may terminate immediately if the other enters liquidation, ceases business, or becomes subject to an insolvency proceeding not dismissed within 60 days, subject to mandatory law.

### 10.3 Chronic Service Failure

Customer's termination rights for repeated service-level failures are stated in the SLS and apply even where individual incidents have been remedied.

### 10.4 Effect of Termination

Customer must pay fees accrued through termination. If Customer terminates for Provider's uncured breach, Provider refunds prepaid unused recurring fees for the terminated service. If Provider terminates for Customer's uncured breach, remaining committed fees become due only if the applicable Order Form expressly states they are accelerated.

## 11. Force Majeure

Neither party is liable for delay caused by events beyond reasonable control, excluding payment obligations. The affected party must promptly notify the other, mitigate effects, and resume performance. If a force-majeure event materially prevents an affected service for 45 consecutive days, either party may terminate that service without penalty. Capacity shortages, subcontractor failure, or lack of funds are not force majeure unless directly caused by a qualifying event despite reasonable continuity measures.

## 12. Notices

Legal notices must be in writing and sent by courier or email to the legal-notice addresses in the applicable Order Form. Email notice is effective only when the sender receives a non-automated acknowledgment from the designated recipient. Operational tickets and account-manager emails are not legal notices.

## 13. Assignment and Change of Control

Neither party may assign the agreement without consent, except to an affiliate or in connection with merger, reorganization, or sale of substantially all assets relating to the agreement, provided the assignee is not a direct competitor of the nonassigning party and can perform the obligations. Customer may terminate within 30 days after notice of an assignment to a direct competitor.

## 14. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution

The agreement is governed by Indian law. Senior executives must attempt good-faith resolution for 15 business days. Unresolved disputes will be finally resolved by arbitration seated in Bengaluru, in English, by one arbitrator. Either party may seek urgent interim relief from a court of competent jurisdiction.

## 15. General

The agreement is the entire agreement on its subject. Invalid provisions will be narrowed to the minimum extent necessary. Failure to enforce is not waiver. Counterparts and electronic signatures are effective. Sections intended by their nature to survive will survive, including payment, confidentiality, intellectual property, liability, dispute resolution, and data return or deletion.
